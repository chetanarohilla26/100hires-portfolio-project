# 30-Day LinkedIn Organic Content Execution Plan

**100Hires Portfolio Assignment — Stage 2**  
**Topic:** LinkedIn Organic Content Strategy for B2B SaaS  
**Author:** Chetana Rohilla

---

# 1. Purpose

This 30-day execution plan converts the LinkedIn Organic Content Playbook into a practical operating system for an early-stage B2B SaaS team.

The objective is **not** to maximize the number of posts published.

Instead, the objective is to determine:

- Which audience problems generate meaningful attention
- Which content formats create useful engagement
- Which points of view attract the right audience
- Which types of proof increase credibility
- Which topics generate conversations with the ideal customer profile (ICP)
- Which content signals are correlated with business outcomes
- Which ideas should be repeated, modified, or discontinued

The plan therefore treats LinkedIn as an **experimentation channel** rather than simply a publishing calendar.

The operating loop is:

> **Research → Hypothesis → Publish → Measure → Learn → Iterate**

---

# 2. Operating Principles

The execution plan follows five principles identified in the Stage 1 research and formalized in the Stage 2 playbook.

## Principle 1 — Relevance Before Reach

A post reaching 100,000 unrelated people may be less valuable than one reaching 1,000 relevant buyers.

The first question is therefore:

> "Did this reach the people we actually want to influence?"

---

## Principle 2 — Proof Before Generic Advice

Whenever possible, content should be grounded in:

1. Customer evidence
2. Product or experiment data
3. First-hand experience
4. Original analysis
5. Expert evidence
6. General industry observations

The further down this list a claim sits, the more carefully it should be framed.

---

## Principle 3 — One Post, One Job

Each post should have one primary objective.

Examples include:

- Teach a framework
- Challenge an assumption
- Demonstrate expertise
- Share an experiment
- Explain a customer problem
- Start a relevant discussion
- Generate a qualified conversation

A post attempting to achieve all of these simultaneously becomes difficult to evaluate.

---

## Principle 4 — Engagement Is a Signal, Not the Final Objective

Likes, comments, and impressions are useful diagnostic signals.

They are not automatically business outcomes.

The plan therefore tracks three levels:

**Attention → Quality → Business**

---

## Principle 5 — The Plan Is a Hypothesis

The first 30 days should generate evidence.

If that evidence contradicts the initial assumptions, the strategy should change.

The goal is not to defend the original plan.

The goal is to learn.

---

# 4. Audience Definition

Before publishing, define one primary audience.

Avoid broad descriptions such as:

> "B2B marketers"

Instead define the audience using:

**Role + Company Stage + Problem**

Examples:

> "Seed-stage B2B SaaS founders struggling with product activation."

> "RevOps leaders at 50–200 employee SaaS companies dealing with inefficient sales handoffs."

---

## 4.1 Audience Hypothesis

Write:

> "I believe [audience] struggles with [problem] because [reason]."

Example:

> "I believe early-stage B2B SaaS founders struggle with activation because they optimize onboarding steps instead of the user's first value moment."

This hypothesis becomes the foundation for the first content experiments.

# 9. Week 1 — Establish the Baseline

## Objective

The first week establishes a baseline across multiple content formats.

The purpose is to collect evidence rather than optimize immediately.

## Day 1 — Define the ICP

### Tasks

1. Select one primary audience.
2. Define their role.
3. Define company stage.
4. Identify their three highest-priority problems.
5. Identify what they currently believe about those problems.
6. Identify what unique expertise the business possesses.

### Output

Create an audience hypothesis:

> "We believe [audience] struggles with [problem] because [reason]."

Collect at least ten potential content problems from sources such as:

- Customer conversations
- Sales objections

# 10. Week 2 — Test Content Variables

## Objective

Week 2 is designed to identify which **content variables** produce the strongest combination of:

- Audience relevance
- Meaningful engagement
- Evidence of expertise
- Business relevance

The objective is not to find the post with the most likes.

Instead, the goal is to determine which content characteristics consistently produce the strongest signals from the target audience.

---

## 10.1 Variables to Test

Test one major variable at a time.

| Variable | Version A | Version B |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Hook | Contrarian claim | Specific observation |
| Content Type | Educational | Story-led |
| Evidence | First-party experience | External research |
| Topic | Broad industry issue | Narrow ICP problem |
| Format | Text post | Carousel |
| CTA | No CTA | Conversation CTA |

Avoid changing multiple major variables in a single experiment.

---

## 10.2 Week 2 Publishing Schedule

| Day | Content Type | Variable Being Tested | Example Topic |
|-----|--------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Day 8 | Educational | Broad vs Specific Topic | "Why SaaS onboarding fails" |
| Day 9 | Story | Story vs Direct Teaching | "Lessons from a failed onboarding experiment" |

| Day 11 | Proof | Evidence vs Generic Advice | "What we learned from reviewing onboarding flows" |

---

## Day 8 — Educational Post

### Objective

Test whether educational content around a specific customer problem produces stronger engagement than broader educational content.

### Example

Weak:

> "Five tips for improving SaaS onboarding."

Improved:

> "Most SaaS onboarding doesn't fail because there are too many steps. It fails because users don't know what success looks like."

### Metrics

Track:

- Impressions
- Saves
- Shares
- Profile visits
- Comments from target accounts

---

## Day 9 — Story-Led Content

Use the following structure:

**Context → Tension → Change → Lesson → Application**

Example:

> We believed users abandoned onboarding because there were too many steps.
>
> We simplified the experience.
>
> Activation barely changed.
>
> After observing users directly, we discovered that the real issue wasn't complexity.
>
> Users simply didn't know what success looked like.
>
> The lesson:
>
> Remove uncertainty before removing steps.

### Compare Against

Publish a second educational post covering the same topic without using a story.

Compare:

- Saves
- Comments
- Profile visits
- Audience quality

---

## Day 10 — Point-of-View Post

Publish a clear opinion supported by reasoning.

Example:

> "Most content calendars solve the wrong problem."

Explain:

1. Why the common approach exists
2. Why you disagree
3. Evidence supporting the alternative
4. Practical recommendation

A contrarian opinion should always be:

- Relevant
- Defensible
- Evidence-based
- Useful

Avoid creating controversy simply to increase engagement.

---

## Day 11 — Proof-Driven Content

Create a post supported by evidence.

Evidence should ideally come from:

1. Customer observations
2. Product data
3. Experiments
4. Real implementation experience
5. Expert research

Example:

> "After reviewing 12 onboarding experiences, we noticed that uncertainty—not the number of steps—was the most common source of friction."

Where evidence is limited, acknowledge that limitation.

Example:

> "This conclusion is based on a small sample and should be treated as a hypothesis rather than a universal rule."

---

## Day 12 — Repurposing Test

Take the strongest insight from the week and present it in another format.

Possible formats:

- Carousel
- Checklist
- Framework
- Document
- Short-form video

Do not copy and paste the original content.

Instead, adapt the same idea for a different format.

# 13. Week 2 Decision Rules

Classify each experiment into one of three categories.

## Keep

Continue using this approach because multiple signals suggest it is effective.

Examples:

- Higher saves
- Better ICP engagement
- More meaningful conversations
- Stronger business relevance

---

## Test Again

Continue testing because the sample size is too small to reach a confident conclusion.

---

## Deprioritize

Reduce emphasis on approaches that consistently produce weak quality or business signals relative to alternatives.

Example:

| Variable | Result | Decision |
|----------|--------|----------|
| Narrow ICP topics | Higher saves and target comments | Keep |
| Generic educational topics | Higher impressions but weak engagement | Deprioritize |
| Story-led posts | Strong discussion but limited sample | Test Again |
| Proof-driven posts | Lower reach but higher profile visits | Keep |
| Carousel format | Higher saves | Test Again |

All decisions should be based on observed evidence rather than assumptions.

- Problem
- Format
- Evidence
- Point of view

Rather than creating entirely new experiments, Week 3 builds upon the strongest findings from earlier tests.

---

## 14.1 Select the Strongest Content Patterns

Review the performance of all content published during Weeks 1 and 2.

Identify the two or three strongest combinations.

Examples:

| Pattern | Why It Performed Well |
|----------|-----------------------|
| Educational + First-party Evidence | High saves and meaningful comments |
| Story + Practical Lesson | High profile visits and discussion |
| Contrarian Point of View + Proof | Strong engagement from target accounts |

Do not rely on impressions alone.

Prioritize combinations that produced evidence of audience relevance.

---

## 14.2 Develop Variations

Avoid reposting the same idea.

Instead, create new content built around the same underlying insight.

### Example

Original insight:

> Customer onboarding fails because users are uncertain about success.

Possible follow-up content:

- Three signs users are confused during onboarding
- Why shorter onboarding does not always improve activation
- The onboarding metric most SaaS teams ignore
- Lessons from observing customer onboarding sessions
- Common onboarding assumptions that deserve to be challenged

The objective is to test whether the underlying insight continues to perform across different formats and angles.

# 15. Week 3 — Audience Feedback Loop

Publishing content creates opportunities to learn from the audience.

Every meaningful interaction should be treated as customer research.

Look for:

- Frequently asked questions
- Objections
- Requests for clarification
- Alternative viewpoints
- Real-world examples shared by readers
- Language consistently used by the audience

Document recurring themes.

These observations become inputs for future content.

---

## Example

Published Post:

> Most onboarding problems are clarity problems.

Comment:

> What if users understand the process but still don't trust the product?

Potential follow-up content:

> Trust is often mistaken for usability during onboarding.

This creates an iterative learning process.

# 27. Final Success Criteria

At the end of the 30-day experiment, the objective is to determine whether the team has gathered enough evidence to make better content decisions.

The experiment should produce:

- A documented baseline
- At least 15–20 content experiments
- A record of content variables tested
- Evidence about audience preferences
- A list of high-potential topics
- A list of low-performing topics
- A shortlist of repeatable content formats
- At least one validated proof-driven content pattern
- At least one repurposed content cluster
- A documented set of unanswered questions
- Three hypotheses for the next 30-day cycle

The experiment should **not** claim to have discovered the "LinkedIn algorithm."

Instead, it should provide evidence about what works for this specific audience, business, and stage of growth.

# 28. Final Principle

The objective of this execution plan is not:

> "Post consistently and hope something goes viral."

The objective is:

> **Research → Form a hypothesis → Publish → Measure → Learn → Improve**

Every publishing cycle should improve the team's understanding of:

- The audience
- Their problems
- Which expertise resonates
- Which evidence builds credibility
- Which content contributes to business outcomes

Content should therefore be treated as an iterative learning process rather than a fixed publishing calendar.

# 29. Post-Experiment Decision Framework

At the end of the experiment, every major content hypothesis should be classified into one of three categories.

## 29.1 Scale

Scale content approaches that consistently demonstrate:

- High audience relevance
- Meaningful engagement
- Strong saves
- Profile visits
- Qualified conversations
- Evidence of business value

### Example

Hypothesis:

> Narrow ICP-specific educational content consistently outperformed broad marketing advice.

Decision:

Increase the proportion of ICP-specific educational content during the next publishing cycle.

---

## 29.2 Continue Testing

Continue testing ideas that show promise but lack sufficient evidence.

Example:

Story-led posts generated stronger discussions, but only two examples were tested.

Decision:

Run another series of controlled experiments before making the approach a permanent part of the strategy.

---

## 29.3 Deprioritize

Reduce emphasis on approaches that repeatedly underperform.

Example:

Generic productivity tips generated impressions but attracted little engagement from the target audience.

Decision:

Allocate future content toward more specific, evidence-backed topics.

# 32. Evidence Discipline

Separate every conclusion into one of three categories.

## Observed

Directly measured.

Example:

> The post received 48 saves.

---

## Interpreted

A conclusion based on observed evidence.

Example:

> The higher save count suggests readers found the framework useful.

---

## Hypothesized

An assumption requiring additional testing.

Example:

> Framework posts may consistently outperform story-led posts.

Hypotheses should never be presented as established facts.

---

# 33. Minimum Evidence Standard

Avoid building strategy from isolated examples.

Whenever possible:

- Repeat successful experiments.
- Test one major variable at a time.
- Compare similar topics.
- Record unsuccessful experiments.
- Distinguish between reach and quality.
- Distinguish between engagement and business outcomes.

Questions to ask after a successful post:

1. Was the audience unusually broad?
2. Was the hook stronger than usual?
3. Did an influential account interact with the post?
4. Was the topic unusually timely?
5. Did the content attract the intended audience?
6. Can the result be reproduced?

Repeatability is a stronger indicator than one exceptional result.

---

# 34. What Not to Optimize For

The following metrics should not become primary objectives:

- Total likes
- Raw impressions
- Follower count
- Viral reach
- Comment volume
- Posting frequency

These indicators may reflect visibility but do not necessarily indicate relevance or commercial value.

A smaller audience of qualified prospects is often more valuable than a much larger audience with no buying intent.

# 35. Content Quality Checklist

Before publishing, review every post using the following checklist.

## Audience

- Is the target audience clearly defined?
- Does the problem matter to that audience?
- Will the intended reader immediately recognize themselves?

## Insight

- Is there one central idea?
- Is the point of view specific?
- Is the insight useful?

## Evidence

- What supports the claim?
- Is the evidence clearly identified?
- Have assumptions been labelled appropriately?

## Value

- Can the reader benefit without clicking elsewhere?
- Is there a practical takeaway?

## Writing

- Is the language simple?
- Can unnecessary sentences be removed?
- Does the writing sound natural?

## Business Relevance

- Does the content reinforce the company's expertise?
- Will it attract the intended audience?
- Could it support future business conversations?

## Integrity

- Are claims supported?
- Are sources cited where appropriate?
- Are limitations acknowledged?

# 38. Reflection

The most valuable outcome of the experiment is not identifying a perfect LinkedIn strategy.

Instead, it is establishing a repeatable process for learning what works for a specific audience.

Several principles consistently emerged throughout the research and experimentation process:

- Audience specificity is more valuable than broad reach.
- Evidence is more persuasive than unsupported opinion.
- Strong points of view require credible proof.
- Native, zero-click value builds trust over time.
- Customer conversations provide a sustainable source of future content ideas.
- Measurement should prioritize business relevance over vanity metrics.

Rather than treating LinkedIn as a platform for broadcasting content, this framework treats it as an ongoing system for:

- Capturing expertise
- Testing hypotheses
- Learning from audience behavior
- Improving future content decisions

# 39. Limitations

The findings documented in this repository should not be interpreted as universal best practices.

Several limitations should be considered.

## Limited Experiment Duration

Thirty days provides directional evidence rather than long-term validation.

Longer observation periods may produce different conclusions.

---

## Sample Size

Individual posts may perform unusually well or poorly due to factors unrelated to content quality.

Multiple iterations are required before establishing confidence in any recommendation.

---

## Platform Changes

LinkedIn's algorithm, user behavior, and content formats continue to evolve.

Recommendations should therefore be revisited periodically rather than treated as permanent rules.

---

## Attribution Challenges

Business outcomes are often influenced by multiple touchpoints.

A LinkedIn post may contribute to awareness or consideration without receiving direct attribution.

Consequently, business impact should be interpreted cautiously.

---

## Audience Differences

Results may differ depending on:

- Industry
- Company size
- Buying cycle
- Existing audience size
- Brand awareness
- Competitive landscape

Future experiments should account for these contextual differences.

# 40. Conclusion

This repository documents a structured approach to designing, testing, and improving LinkedIn organic content for early-stage B2B SaaS teams.

Rather than relying on templates or assumptions, the proposed system emphasizes:

- Understanding a clearly defined audience.
- Identifying meaningful customer problems.
- Developing evidence-backed points of view.
- Publishing useful standalone content.
- Measuring both qualitative and quantitative signals.
- Iterating based on observed evidence.

The operating loop remains intentionally simple:

```text
Research
    ↓
Form Hypothesis
    ↓
Create Content
    ↓
Publish
    ↓
Measure
    ↓
Learn
    ↓
Iterate
```

Each iteration should improve the team's understanding of:

- The audience.
- Their problems.
- Which expertise is valued.
- Which evidence builds credibility.
- Which content contributes to meaningful business outcomes.

The objective is not to maximize posting frequency or pursue viral reach.

The objective is to build a repeatable system that transforms real customer understanding into credible, useful, and commercially relevant content.

By documenting assumptions, experiments, observations, and learnings, the repository becomes more than a collection of marketing recommendations.

It becomes an evolving knowledge base that supports continuous improvement in LinkedIn organic content strategy for B2B SaaS.
