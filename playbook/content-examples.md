# LinkedIn Organic Content — B2B SaaS Content Examples

**100Hires Portfolio Assignment — Stage 2**  
**Topic:** LinkedIn Organic Content Strategy for B2B SaaS  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate how the playbook can be translated into practical LinkedIn content.

---

# 1. How to Use These Examples

The examples below are not presented as guaranteed high-performing posts.

They are practical examples of how the principles in the playbook can be applied to B2B SaaS content.

The examples deliberately use different content objectives:

- Education
- Point of view
- Customer insight
- Storytelling
- Proof
- Product/feature education
- Demand generation
- Experimentation

The objective is to demonstrate the **reasoning behind the content**, not simply provide templates to copy.

# 2. Example 1 — Customer Insight Post

### Objective

Turn repeated customer conversations into useful content.

### Target audience

B2B SaaS founders and product leaders.

### Content thesis

> Many onboarding problems are actually uncertainty problems rather than usability problems.

### Evidence

Assume the company has observed this pattern through customer conversations and onboarding-session reviews.

The example does **not** invent a numerical result because no actual company data is available.

### Example Post

> Your onboarding problem might not be onboarding.
>
> We initially thought users were dropping because there were too many steps.
>
> So the obvious solution was:
>
> Remove steps.
>
> Make the flow shorter.
>
> Reduce friction.
>
> But after watching users go through the process, we noticed something different.
>
> They weren't necessarily confused by the number of steps.
>
> They were confused about what "done" looked like.
>
> That changed the question.
>
> Instead of asking:
>
> "How do we make onboarding shorter?"
>
> We started asking:
>
> "How quickly can the user understand what success looks like?"
>
> Three things became more important:
>
> 1. Make the first successful outcome obvious.
> 2. Show users what progress looks like.
> 3. Remove uncertainty before removing steps.
>
> Shorter onboarding isn't automatically better onboarding.
>
> Sometimes the fastest way to improve activation is to make the destination clearer.

### Why This Fits the Playbook

**Audience:** SaaS teams responsible for activation.

**Attention:** Challenges the assumption that onboarding problems are caused by too many steps.

**Proof:** Uses an observed customer/product pattern.

**Teaching:** Provides three practical principles.

**Application:** Gives the reader a decision rule for diagnosing onboarding problems.

### Source Basis

This example applies the playbook's:

- Problem Bank
- Expertise → Evidence → Application framework
- Proof-driven content principle

The specific scenario is an illustrative example, not a documented company result.

# 3. Example 2 — Educational Framework

### Objective

Teach a practical framework without requiring the reader to click elsewhere.

### Target audience

B2B SaaS marketers.

### Content thesis

> A good SaaS content idea should pass three tests: relevance, evidence, and usefulness.

### Example Post

> Before publishing another B2B SaaS post, ask three questions.
>
> **1. Is it relevant?**
>
> Does this solve a problem your target customer actually has?
>
> If the answer is "probably," you haven't researched the problem enough.
>
> **2. Do you have evidence?**
>
> Why should anyone believe you?
>
> Evidence could be:
>
> - Customer conversations
> - Product data
> - An experiment
> - A case study
> - Your own experience
> - Original analysis
>
> **3. Is it useful?**
>
> Can someone do something differently after reading it?
>
> If the reader finishes the post thinking:
>
> "Interesting."
>
> but not:
>
> "I know what to do next."
>
> the post probably needs more application.
>
> My simple content test:
>
> **Relevance → Evidence → Application**
>
> If one is missing, strengthen it before publishing.

### Why This Fits the Playbook

This directly demonstrates the original:

**Expertise → Evidence → Application**

framework developed in the playbook.

# 4. Example 3 — Point-of-View Post

### Objective

Build authority through a defensible opinion.

### Target audience

B2B SaaS marketing leaders.

### Content thesis

> B2B SaaS teams should not optimize LinkedIn content primarily for likes.

### Example Post

> Your best LinkedIn post might be the one that gets 12 likes.
>
> That sounds wrong.
>
> But consider what you actually want from B2B content.
>
> Not applause.
>
> You want:
>
> - The right people to notice you.
> - The right people to trust you.
> - Conversations with potential customers.
> - Future buyers to remember your company.
>
> A post can get thousands of impressions and still accomplish none of those things.
>
> Meanwhile, a post seen by 200 people might generate:
>
> 3 meaningful conversations  
> 2 profile visits from target accounts  
> 1 sales conversation
>
> I'd rather have the second outcome.
>
> That doesn't mean engagement is useless.
>
> Engagement tells you whether people are paying attention.
>
> It just shouldn't be the final definition of success.
>
> For B2B SaaS, I'd measure content in three layers:
>
> **Attention → Quality → Business signal**
>
> Likes live in the first layer.
>
> They shouldn't determine whether the strategy is working.

### Why This Fits the Playbook

This applies the distinction between:

- Attention metrics
- Quality metrics
- Business metrics

It also reflects the disagreement identified between Justin Welsh's attention-oriented framework and Chris Walker's emphasis on commercial outcomes.

# 5. Example 4 — Story + Lesson

### Objective

Use storytelling to communicate a business lesson.

### Target audience

SaaS founders and product marketers.

### Content thesis

> A failed experiment can produce more useful content than a successful one.

### Example Post

> We spent two weeks building the experiment.
>
> The result?
>
> It didn't work.
>
> At first, that felt like a waste.
>
> Then we asked a different question:
>
> "What did this failure teach us that we wouldn't have learned otherwise?"
>
> We found three things:
>
> **1. Our assumption was wrong.**
>
> We believed users wanted feature X.
>
> The feedback showed they actually wanted outcome Y.
>
> **2. We measured the wrong signal.**
>
> We were looking at clicks.
>
> The more useful signal was whether users completed the next meaningful action.
>
> **3. The failure exposed a better question.**
>
> Instead of:
>
> "Will users click this?"
>
> we should have asked:
>
> "Does this help users reach value faster?"
>
> The experiment failed.
>
> The learning didn't.
>
> That's why failed experiments are often worth documenting.
>
> They show the reasoning behind the decision — not just the final result.

### Why This Fits the Playbook

Structure:

**Context → Tension → What changed → Lesson → Application**

The example also demonstrates proof-driven content without inventing performance numbers.

# 6. Example 5 — Product Teardown

### Objective

Demonstrate expertise through original analysis.

### Target audience

Product marketers and SaaS founders.

### Content thesis

> A product's onboarding flow should communicate the value milestone before explaining every feature.

### Example Post

> I reviewed 10 SaaS onboarding flows this week.
>
> One pattern stood out:
>
> Many products explain the product before showing users the outcome.
>
> That's backwards.
>
> A new user doesn't necessarily want to understand everything your product can do.
>
> They want to know:
>
> **"What will I be able to accomplish after I finish this?"**
>
> A stronger onboarding sequence is:
>
> **Outcome → First action → Feedback → Expansion**
>
> Instead of:
>
> Feature → Feature → Feature → Feature
>
> The difference is subtle.
>
> But it changes the user's mental model.
>
> Don't make the user learn your product before they experience its value.
>
> Let the value teach them why the product matters.

### Evidence Note

If this were published as a real post, the author should document:

- Which 10 products were reviewed
- Selection criteria
- Date of analysis
- Screenshots or links where appropriate
- What specifically was observed

The number "10" should only be used when the analysis has actually been conducted.

# 7. Example 6 — Zero-Click Educational Post

### Objective

Give the reader complete value directly inside LinkedIn.

### Target audience

Early-stage B2B SaaS marketers.

### Content thesis

> Most early-stage SaaS teams don't need more content ideas. They need better sources of ideas.

### Example Post

> Stop brainstorming LinkedIn topics.
>
> Start collecting customer problems.
>
> Your next 20 posts are probably already hiding inside:
>
> **Sales calls**
>
> What objections keep appearing?
>
> **Customer support**
>
> What questions keep getting asked?
>
> **Onboarding**
>
> Where do users get stuck?
>
> **Lost deals**
>
> Why did prospects choose another solution?
>
> **Product requests**
>
> What are customers repeatedly asking for?
>
> **Customer reviews**
>
> What language do customers use to describe their problem?
>
> Turn those observations into:
>
> Problem → Insight → Lesson → Post
>
> Your customers are already telling you what they want to read.
>
> You just need a system for capturing it.

### Why This Fits the Playbook

This demonstrates:

- Problem Bank
- Audience-first content
- Zero-click value
- Customer-driven content ideation

# 8. Example 7 — AI-Assisted Content

### Objective

Show how AI can improve content operations without replacing expertise.

### Target audience

B2B SaaS marketing teams.

### Example Post

> AI can write your LinkedIn post.
>
> That's exactly why you shouldn't let it decide what your post says.
>
> Use AI for:
>
> - Organizing interview notes
> - Finding recurring themes
> - Generating headline variations
> - Turning transcripts into possible content ideas
> - Repurposing an existing insight
>
> Don't use it to:
>
> - Invent customer stories
> - Create fake results
> - Manufacture opinions
> - Replace subject-matter expertise
>
> My preferred workflow:
>
> **Human experience**
>
> ↓
>
> **AI organization**
>
> ↓
>
> **Human judgment**
>
> ↓
>
> **Human edit**
>
> ↓
>
> **Publish**
>
> The advantage isn't producing more AI content.
>
> It's using AI to spend more time on the thinking that AI cannot provide.

### Source Basis

This example applies the disagreement between:

- Justin Welsh's practical use of AI for content workflows
- Dave Gerhardt's emphasis on human experience and authenticity

Sources are documented in the main playbook.

# 9. Example 8 — Demand Generation Without a Hard CTA

### Objective

Build demand without turning every post into a sales pitch.

### Target audience

B2B SaaS decision-makers.

### Example Post

> Most B2B SaaS content tries to convert too early.
>
> Someone reads one useful post.
>
> Immediately:
>
> "Book a demo."
>
> But the reader may not even trust you yet.
>
> A better sequence is:
>
> **Teach → Demonstrate → Build trust → Create relevance → Convert**
>
> A content post doesn't need to generate a lead immediately to be commercially useful.
>
> It can:
>
> - Introduce a problem
> - Change how someone thinks about it
> - Demonstrate expertise
> - Create familiarity
> - Give the buyer a reason to remember you
>
> Then, when the problem becomes urgent, your company is already familiar.
>
> Sometimes the best CTA is simply:
>
> "Here's what we learned."
>
> And then letting the reader decide whether they want more.

### Why This Fits the Playbook

This demonstrates the playbook's position on:

- Zero-click value
- Demand generation
- Non-promotional content
- Trust before conversion

# 10. Example 9 — Content Repurposing

### Objective

Turn one strong insight into multiple assets.

### Source Insight

Suppose a SaaS founder discovers through customer conversations that prospects consistently misunderstand a product category.

### Original Insight

> "Customers don't understand the category because companies explain features instead of the underlying problem."

### Repurposing System

#### LinkedIn Post

Explain the observation and provide three examples.

#### LinkedIn Carousel

Slide 1:
"The biggest SaaS messaging mistake"

Slides 2–6:
Examples of feature-led vs problem-led messaging.

#### Newsletter

Expand the idea into a deeper analysis.

#### Short Video

Founder explains the observation in 60–90 seconds.

#### Sales Enablement

Create a one-page messaging framework for sales representatives.

#### Customer Education

Turn the insight into a help-center or educational article.

### Principle

The goal is not to copy-paste one post across platforms.

The goal is:

**One insight → multiple useful formats**

This follows the distribution and repurposing principles identified in the Stage 1 research.

# 11. Example 10 — Experimental Post

### Objective

Use LinkedIn as a learning system rather than only a distribution channel.

### Hypothesis

Posts containing first-party evidence will generate more meaningful engagement from the target audience than generic educational posts.

### Test

Publish:

- 3 evidence-led posts
- 3 generic educational posts

Keep the audience and topic area reasonably consistent.

### Track

- Impressions
- Saves
- Meaningful comments
- Target-account engagement
- Profile visits
- Inbound conversations

### Decision

If evidence-led posts consistently generate stronger quality or business signals, increase their share in the content mix.

If not, investigate:

- Topic selection
- Audience relevance
- Hook quality
- Evidence strength
- Distribution

### Important

Do not conclude that "proof-based content doesn't work" from six posts.

The experiment should generate a signal, not pretend to establish a universal law.

# 12. Content Format Decision Tree

Use this before deciding what format to publish.

```text
Do we have first-party evidence?
│
├── YES
│   │
│   ├── Is there a story?
│   │      ├── YES → Story + Evidence + Lesson
│   │      └── NO → Case Study / Experiment / Teardown
│   │
│   └── Is the insight highly practical?
│          ├── YES → Framework / Checklist
│          └── NO → Point of View / Observation
│
└── NO
    │
    ├── Do we have strong expert evidence?
    │      ├── YES → Educational synthesis
    │      └── NO → Conduct more research
    │
    └── Avoid presenting assumptions as facts.
